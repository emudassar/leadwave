## The gap between interested and gone

Someone asks for the link at 11pm, gets it, means to look in the morning, and
forgets. They did not decide against you. They just never came back. A single
reminder recovers a meaningful share of those people, and the ones it does not
recover were never going to buy.

## One, not seven

LeadWave sends exactly one follow-up. There is no drip sequence, no five-touch
cadence, no "value email" three days later. Partly because the second nudge
converts almost nobody and annoys everybody, and partly because Messenger is a
personal inbox and people treat it that way.

## How it knows to stay quiet

The nudge is cancelled if the contact replies or taps the link. That check
happens twice: when the event comes in, and again at send time. The second check
is the one that matters — schedules drift, webhooks arrive late, and a reminder
that arrives two minutes after they already bought is the worst message you can
send.

## Timing

Anywhere from 30 minutes to 20 hours. The cap is deliberate: Messenger closes the
standard window 24 hours after their last message, so a nudge scheduled beyond
that would be held rather than delivered. Ninety minutes is a good default — long
enough that they have genuinely moved on, short enough that they still remember
asking.

## Re-attach the link

Turn on "resend the original link" and the reminder carries it again, so they do
not have to scroll back through the thread to find what they were looking for.
