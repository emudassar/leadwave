## The forty-times-a-week question

Every business has one. "Do you deliver to Multan?" "What time do you open?" "Is
this still available?" You have typed the answer so many times you could do it
in your sleep — which is roughly when the next one arrives.

## Set the words people actually type

Not the words you would use. Real people type *shiping*, *delivry*, *how much
for delivery*, and half of them in Roman Urdu. Add all of them. Matching ignores
case and punctuation, and you choose how strict it is:

- **Contains** — the forgiving default. The word anywhere in the message fires it.
- **Exact** — the whole message has to be the keyword. Useful for a one-word code.
- **Starts with** — good when people lead with the keyword and then keep typing.

## The exclusion list is the important bit

*Shipping* should fire your shipping answer. *My shipment is lost* absolutely
should not. Add exclusion words and those messages are left alone for a person to
handle — which is what someone with a lost parcel deserves.

## Give them somewhere to go next

Add quick-reply chips under the answer — "Track an order", "Talk to a human",
"See the catalogue". They are tappable, they disappear once one is tapped, and
they turn a dead-end answer into a conversation that keeps moving.

## When a human should take over

Reply yourself in the LeadWave inbox and the AI stands down in that thread for 48
hours. The automation does not talk over you, and the person does not get a bot
reply three seconds after a real answer.
