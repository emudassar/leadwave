## Forms leak

The standard funnel is: comment, click the link, load the landing page, read the
form, type your email, submit. Every one of those steps loses people, and on a
phone with a bad connection it loses a lot of them.

## Ask in the conversation instead

They are already in a chat with you. Ask there. Messenger shows a chip that sends
the email address on their profile — one tap, no typing, no page load, no
keyboard. Completion rates are not in the same league as a form.

## What lands in your contacts

Not just an address. Each lead is stored with:

- **The automation that captured it**, so you can tell which rule earns its place
- **The post it came from**, when the trigger was a comment
- **The raw value they gave** as well as the normalised one, so support can see
  what actually happened

## The consent question, handled honestly

Someone tapping a chip to send you their email is giving it to you — but that is
not the same as signing up for a daily newsletter. Say what you will send them in
the message that asks. "Drop your email and I'll send the code" earns you one
email's worth of permission, and people who feel tricked unsubscribe from
everything.

## Phone numbers too

Same flow, same chip. Numbers are parsed and normalised to E.164 before they are
stored, so you do not end up with four formats of the same number and a
deduplication problem six months from now.

## Getting it out again

Export to CSV whenever you like, or, on the Business plan, connect Google Sheets
and every lead appends itself to a spreadsheet the moment it is captured —
attribution included, so the export is still useful a month later.
