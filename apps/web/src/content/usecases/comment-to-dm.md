## The problem with a good post

A post does well. Forty people comment "price". You answer the first eight, get
distracted, and the other thirty-two never hear back. They were the warmest
leads you had that week, and by the time you come back to the thread they have
moved on.

## What the automation does

You set one rule: when a comment on this Page contains *price*, *pricing* or
*how much*, do two things.

**Reply publicly under the comment.** Something short — "Just sent it your way
📩". This matters more than it looks. Everyone else reading the comments sees a
business that answers, and a few of them comment too.

**Send the real thing privately.** The price list, the link, the booking page —
whatever the actual answer is — arrives in their Messenger inbox within seconds
of them commenting.

## The rules it keeps for you

Facebook has opinions about this, and breaking them is how Pages get restricted.
LeadWave enforces them so you do not have to think about it:

- **One private reply per comment, ever.** Recorded in a ledger that survives
  restarts, re-runs and Facebook redelivering the same webhook three times.
- **Once per commenter per post.** If the same person comments four times, they
  get one message.
- **Never your own comments.** The Page replying to itself is both embarrassing
  and against the rules.
- **A shared send budget.** If a post goes viral, all your automations draw from
  the same per-Page budget, so you cannot burn through your rate limits.

## Rotate the public reply

Four hundred identical public replies read like a bot, because they are one.
Write three or four variants and LeadWave picks between them at random. It is a
small thing that makes the comments section look like a person is there.

## What you find out afterwards

Every link in the message becomes a tracked short link automatically. You get
clicks, unique clickers, and which automation sent them — so "the Reel did well"
becomes "the Reel sent 212 people to the price list and 41 of them opened it".
