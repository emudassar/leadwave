## Where the money leaks

Small shops on Facebook lose sales in two places: the comment nobody answered,
and the question nobody was awake for. Both are fixable with one rule each.

## Start here

**Comment → price list.** Set *price*, *pricing*, *how much* and *rate* as
keywords on every post. The public reply tells the rest of the comments section
that you answer; the private message delivers the actual list.

**Keyword → shipping answer.** Set *ship*, *shipping*, *delivery* and *deliver*,
with *shipment lost* on the exclusion list so a complaint reaches a person.

**Story reply → best sellers.** Every reply to a story becomes a three-card
carousel of what you want to move this week.

## Then read the numbers

After a fortnight the carousel tells you which product people tap, not just that
somebody tapped. That is a merchandising decision you can make on Monday: lead
with the hoodie, find out why the cap is not landing, and drop the card nobody
has touched.

## Restock alerts without a mailing list tool

Add an email capture step to the automation that fires when something is out of
stock. "Want to know when it's back? Drop your email" — one tap, straight into
your contacts, exportable whenever you want to send the alert.

## Order status stays human

Do not automate "where is my order". The automation does not know, and a
confident wrong answer about a late parcel costs more than the time it saves.
Put those words on the exclusion list.
